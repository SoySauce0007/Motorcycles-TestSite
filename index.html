The Definitive Guide to Retro Motorcycles

Classic soul • Modern reliability • Timeless style

There is something deeply satisfying about a motorcycle that looks like it rolled off the line in 1965 but starts on the first press of a button. Modern retro motorcycles offer exactly that: the sculpted tanks, round headlights, spoked wheels, and naked soul of a vintage bike — wrapped around contemporary engines, brakes, and electronics that make them genuinely practical to own and ride every day.

The seven machines profiled below represent the best of the breed. Each one has been evaluated for reliability, affordability, and overall rider appeal. Whether you're a first-time buyer or a seasoned rider looking for a soulful second bike, one of these is likely your answer.

01
Royal Enfield • Interceptor 650 • 2018 – Present

The People's Classic

The Interceptor 650 is arguably the greatest value in retro motorcycling today. Styled after the beloved 1960s British parallel-twin roadsters, it pairs honest, chrome-kissed looks with a smooth, rev-happy twin that is refreshingly easy to live with.

Engine

648cc parallel-twin, air/oil-cooled, SOHC

Power

47 hp @ 7,250 rpm

Torque

38 lb-ft @ 5,250 rpm

Weight

430 lbs (195 kg) wet

Seat Height

31.5 in (800 mm)

Fuel Capacity

3.5 gal (13.7 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Modern engine, low maintenance schedule
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$5,500–$6,200 new — remarkable value
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ Huge community & aftermarket scene

Verdict: If budget is your primary concern and you still want genuine twin-cylinder charm, nothing comes close. The Interceptor 650 is fun, handsome, and cheap to own.

02
Triumph • Bonneville T120 • 2016 – Present

The Icon Reborn

The name "Bonneville" has meant one thing since 1959: the apex of British motorcycle style. The modern T120 honours that legacy with a torque-rich 1200cc parallel-twin, impeccable fit and finish, and ride-by-wire refinements hidden beneath vintage-perfect bodywork.

Engine

1200cc parallel-twin, liquid-cooled, SOHC

Power

79 hp @ 6,550 rpm

Torque

80 lb-ft @ 3,500 rpm

Weight

462 lbs (210 kg) wet

Seat Height

30.5 in (775 mm)

Fuel Capacity

3.6 gal (13.6 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Modern Hinckley engineering, excellent long-term record
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$12,000–$13,500 new — premium pricing
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ The benchmark every retro bike is judged against

Verdict: The Bonneville T120 is the gold standard. If you can afford it, it will reward you with decades of pleasure and exceptional resale value.

03
Kawasaki • W800 • 2011 – Present

The Gentleman Rider

Kawasaki's W800 is a quiet masterpiece. Its air-cooled 773cc bevel-drive parallel-twin draws direct inspiration from the 1966 W1 — the machine that proved Japan could match Britain at its own game. Chrome tank badges, peashooter exhausts, and spoked wheels make this one of the most visually authentic retro bikes on the market.

Engine

773cc parallel-twin, air-cooled, SOHC, bevel-drive cams

Power

47 hp @ 6,500 rpm

Torque

44 lb-ft @ 4,800 rpm

Weight

466 lbs (211 kg) wet

Seat Height

30.9 in (785 mm)

Fuel Capacity

3.7 gal (14.1 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Kawasaki build quality is industry-leading
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$9,000–$9,500 new — fair for the quality
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ Understated and deeply authentic

Verdict: The W800 is the most mechanically authentic retro bike money can buy. It rewards patient, smooth riding and never lets you forget what motorcycles are about.

04
Honda • CB1100 • 2013 – 2020

The Four-Cylinder Heirloom

When Honda wanted to build the definitive modern classic, they went straight to their greatest legacy: the CB750 inline-four that changed motorcycling in 1969. The CB1100 scales that formula up to 1140cc, wraps it in late-1970s styling, and delivers it with the bulletproof reliability only Honda can guarantee.

Engine

1140cc inline-four, air-cooled, DOHC

Power

87 hp @ 7,500 rpm

Torque

63 lb-ft @ 5,500 rpm

Weight

536 lbs (243 kg) wet

Seat Height

31.1 in (790 mm)

Fuel Capacity

4.2 gal (16 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Honda's reputation for durability is unmatched
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$6,000–$9,000 used; discontinued new
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ Four-cylinder soundtrack is uniquely compelling

Verdict: The CB1100 is the retro bike for the rider who values reliability above all else. It is heavy but comfortable, fast but forgiving, and will simply never let you down.

05
Ducati • Scrambler Icon • 2015 – Present

The Italian Adventurer

The Ducati Scrambler Icon recaptured the free-spirited DNA of the original 1962 Ducati Scrambler and handed it to a new generation. Its 803cc Desmodue L-twin produces generous torque with a distinct voice, and its lightweight chassis makes every road feel playful. The trade-off is that Ducati's Desmodromic valve system requires specialist service.

Engine

803cc L-twin (Desmodue), air-cooled, SOHC Desmo

Power

73 hp @ 8,250 rpm

Torque

49 lb-ft @ 5,750 rpm

Weight

370 lbs (168 kg) wet

Seat Height

31.1 in (790 mm)

Fuel Capacity

3.4 gal (13.5 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Desmo valve service every 7,500 mi adds cost
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$9,000–$10,500 new; ownership costs add up
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ Lightest, most nimble, most exciting of the group

Verdict: For riders who prioritise fun and style over economy, the Scrambler Icon is irresistible. Budget for the Desmo service intervals and you will be rewarded with one of the most exciting retro bikes available.

06
BMW Motorrad • R nineT • 2014 – Present

The German Thoroughbred

BMW created the R nineT to celebrate 90 years of boxer engines, and the result is one of the most accomplished motorcycles ever made. The 1170cc air/oil-cooled boxer produces 109 hp with a distinctive side-to-side rocking motion at idle. It is also extensively customisable — BMW designed it to be a platform for personal expression.

Engine

1170cc boxer-twin, air/oil-cooled, OHV, DOHC

Power

109 hp @ 7,750 rpm

Torque

85 lb-ft @ 6,000 rpm

Weight

492 lbs (223 kg) wet

Seat Height

31.3 in (795 mm)

Fuel Capacity

4.2 gal (17 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ BMW engineering; long service intervals
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$14,500–$16,500 new — the premium option
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ Most powerful & most customisable of the group

Verdict: The R nineT is the enthusiast's choice — the bike for someone who wants the most performance, the best engineering, and the most room to personalise. Its price is high, but it delivers full value.

07
Moto Guzzi • V7 • 2012 – Present

The Italian Soul

No motorcycle carries its heritage more visibly than the Moto Guzzi V7. The transverse V-twin engine — flanking out from the frame like a pair of chrome-capped knuckles — is the most distinctive silhouette in motorcycling. The V7 is languid, characterful, and deeply Italian in every way.

Engine

744cc transverse V-twin, air-cooled, OHV

Power

52 hp @ 6,200 rpm

Torque

44 lb-ft @ 4,900 rpm

Weight

418 lbs (190 kg) wet

Seat Height

30.5 in (775 mm)

Fuel Capacity

4.2 gal (16 L)

Reliability ★★★★★ Solid modern build; find a good Guzzi specialist
Affordability ★★★★★ ~$8,500–$9,500 new — excellent mid-range value
Rider Appeal ★★★★★ The most visually unique machine in this guide

Verdict: The Moto Guzzi V7 is for the rider who wants something truly different. Its low seat, relaxed power delivery, and singular looks make it one of the most pleasurable bikes to own — not despite its Italian quirks, but because of them.
